# Client Call Prep — Hire'in Solutions

## Before the Call (5-Minute Prep Checklist)

1. **Research the company**: Current open roles on LinkedIn/Indeed, recent news, funding status, headcount trends
2. **Identify their likely pain**: High job posting volume = urgency; niche roles = sourcing challenge; regulated industry = compliance burden
3. **Know their staffing model preference**: Are they a contract shop or direct-hire focused? Check Glassdoor/Levels.fyi for clues
4. **Prepare a relevant proof point**: Pick one case study from the matching domain
5. **Set your call goal**: Discovery call goal = uncover 1 live role and agree on next step; not to sell everything

---

## Discovery Call Structure (30-Minute Format)

### Minutes 0–3: Open Strong
> "Thanks for making time, [Name]. I did some homework before this call — I noticed [personalized observation about their hiring/company]. I'd love to understand more about what's happening on your end. Do you mind if I ask a few questions first?"

This earns trust and avoids launching into a pitch too early.

### Minutes 3–15: Discovery Questions

**Role & Volume**
- "What roles are hardest to fill right now, and how long have they been open?"
- "How many positions are you trying to fill in the next 60–90 days?"
- "Are these contract, permanent, or a mix?"

**Current Process**
- "Walk me through your current sourcing process. What's working, what isn't?"
- "How many vendors are you working with right now, and how do you evaluate them?"
- "What does a good submission look like to your team — what makes you want to interview someone?"

**Pain & Urgency**
- "What happens to the business if these roles stay open another 60 days?"
- "Have you had bad hires recently — candidates who looked good on paper but didn't work out?"
- "Is there a compliance or credential requirement that's been adding friction to your hiring?"

**Budget & Decision**
- "Do you have an approved headcount and budget for these roles, or are we at the exploration stage?"
- "Who else would be involved in evaluating a new staffing partner?"

### Minutes 15–22: Tailored Pitch

Only pitch after you've listened. Tailor to what you heard:

> "Based on what you've shared, here's where I think we can help specifically: [2–3 targeted points matching their pain to Hire'in proof points]."

Always include:
- The relevant technology (KleriQ.ai or CredentialRx.ai)
- One proof metric that maps to their pain
- The 24-hour first submission commitment

### Minutes 22–28: Handle Objections

See objection scripts below.

### Minutes 28–30: Close with a Specific Next Step

Never end with "let me know if you want to move forward." Always suggest:
> "Let's do this: send me the JD for [most urgent role] today, and I'll have pre-screened candidates in your inbox within 24 hours. That way you can evaluate us on a real role, not just a conversation."

## Objection Handling Scripts

### "We already have preferred vendors / a PSL."

> "Totally understand — most of our best clients came to us while they had a PSL. The question isn't whether to replace your existing vendors; it's whether they're filling your hardest roles fast enough. Which roles in your current pipeline are your vendors struggling with? We'd be happy to take on one role — just as a trial — and let results speak for us."

**Why this works**: Doesn't challenge their existing relationships; positions HIS as an add-on for hard-to-fill roles, which is where PSLs always have gaps.

---

### "Your rates are too high."

> "I appreciate you being direct. Can I ask what range you're expecting? I want to make sure we're comparing apples to apples. Our bill rates reflect pre-vetted candidates who've been scored by our AI and interviewed by a recruiter — so you're not paying for resumes that don't pass your phone screen. If you've had to reject 80% of what other vendors send, our effective cost-per-hire is often lower, not higher."

**Follow-up if they push**: "Would it help if I showed you the math on total cost of a bad hire vs. our placement fee? It usually changes the conversation."

---

### "We use an MSP / VMS."

> "No problem — we're set up to work through VMS portals. We've placed candidates through [Fieldglass/Beeline/Workday/etc.] before. Which VMS are you on? I can confirm we're connected or walk through the onboarding if needed. The process is the same on our end — you still get 24-hour submissions and the same candidate quality."

**Key point**: Don't let the VMS objection end the conversation. Most MSPs allow new vendors to register. Offer to handle the paperwork.

---

### "We're not hiring right now."

> "Understood — timing matters. A few questions: Is it a budget hold, a headcount freeze, or just this quarter? And when hiring does open back up, what roles tend to come first? The reason I ask is that the best time to set up a staffing partner is before you need one — so when the req opens, we're already at your speed."

**Leave-behind**: Offer to send the capability deck and one relevant case study so they have your info when timing shifts.

---

### "We only use direct sourcing / internal recruiting."

> "That's actually a great signal — it means you're invested in quality, not just volume. A lot of our best partnerships are with companies that use their internal team for most roles but bring us in for time-sensitive or hard-to-fill positions. We don't displace internal recruiters — we extend your capacity when you need it."

---

### "We had a bad experience with a staffing firm before."

> "I'm sorry to hear that — it's more common than it should be in this industry. Can you tell me what went wrong? Was it the candidate quality, the communication, the compliance side? I want to understand what 'good' looks like for you so I can show you exactly how we do it differently. Our 95% retention rate comes from being selective about who we submit — we'd rather take longer to find the right person than flood you with resumes."

---

### "We need someone with [very specific niche skill]."

> "Niche roles are actually where we add the most value. Our AI platform, KleriQ.ai, was built specifically for exactly this problem — it turns a complex JD into precise sourcing logic so we're not searching blind. For [specific skill], here's how I'd approach it: [brief sourcing rationale]. Let me pull our active pipeline for that profile and get back to you by end of day."

## Talking Points Bank (Modular)

Use these as standalone talking points in any conversation:

- **Speed**: *"First submissions in 24 hours — not 24 business days."*
- **Quality**: *"Our AI scores every candidate before a human ever looks at them. You only see the top tier."*
- **Retention**: *"95% of our clients renew. That's not marketing — it's because the placements work."*
- **Compliance**: *"For healthcare, we run automated license and exclusion checks against all 50-state public directories. Every candidate arrives credential-complete."*
- **Scale**: *"We cover all 50 US states. Whether you need one person or a full team, same process, same SLA."*
- **Risk reduction**: *"We offer a replacement guarantee on direct hires. If it's not working in [60/90] days, we restart the search at no additional fee."*
- **Technology**: *"We didn't license an ATS and call it AI. We built KleriQ.ai ourselves — it's purpose-built for staffing, not repurposed from another industry."*

## Post-Call Protocol

Within 2 hours of a positive discovery call:
1. Send a recap email: 3 bullets of what you heard + proposed next step
2. Attach the domain-relevant capability deck or case study
3. Set a calendar invite for the follow-up (don't leave it open-ended)
4. Log in CRM with: company, contact, pain point, role type, decision timeline, next action date
