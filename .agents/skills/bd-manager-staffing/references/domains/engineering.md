## Buyer Personas

### Director of Engineering / VP of Operations

**What they care about**: Project delivery timelines, skill depth, reducing ramp time for new hires, managing contractor cost vs. permanent headcount budgets.

**Pain points**:
- Generalist agencies that don't understand engineering disciplines
- Long lead times for niche engineering skills
- Project stalls due to unfilled roles
- Inconsistency in technical screening — candidates pass the agency but fail the client's practical test

**How to approach**:
- Demonstrate domain knowledge — use engineering terminology correctly
- Mention that HIS uses domain-specialist recruiters, not generalists
- Focus on KleriQ.ai's JD-to-sourcing logic for technical role precision

---

### Plant Manager / Manufacturing Director

**What they care about**: Line uptime, safety compliance, production targets, cost-per-hire for skilled trades and engineering roles.

**Pain points**:
- Skilled trades and process engineers are hard to find in most regional markets
- Turnover on the manufacturing floor disrupts production
- Safety compliance requires verified competencies, not just a resume claim

**How to approach**:
- Lead with reliability and technical competency verification
- Emphasize regional market coverage and local candidate sourcing (manufacturing plants are geography-bound)
- Offer contract-to-hire as a risk-reduction model

---

### HR Business Partner / Talent Acquisition Lead (Engineering org)

**What they care about**: Time-to-fill, quality of submissions, vendor management, DEI pipeline considerations.

**Pain points**:
- TA teams rarely have deep engineering domain expertise
- Hiring managers complain that TA sends generic resumes
- Long feedback loops between TA and engineering managers

**How to approach**:
- Position HIS as the domain-specialist extension of their TA team
- Offer to provide detailed candidate profiles with technical competency notes, not just a resume PDF
- Set up a direct recruiter-to-hiring-manager feedback channel to shorten iteration loops

## Compliance Notes (Engineering)

**Licensing verification:**
- Professional Engineer (PE) license: verify with NCEES and relevant state board for licensed roles
- OSHA 30-hour construction/general industry: collect certificate of completion
- TWIC (Transportation Worker ID Credential): required for maritime/port-adjacent roles

**Security / defense considerations:**
- DoD project roles may require active security clearance — source time doubles for cleared engineers
- ITAR compliance: some manufacturing roles require US Person status (citizen or green card)

**Background checks:**
- Standard 7-year criminal + employment verification
- Motor vehicle record (MVR) for roles involving heavy equipment or company vehicles
- Drug screen: construction and manufacturing sites typically require 5-panel minimum

## Market Context (Engineering)

**Growth areas to emphasize in outreach:**
- **Infrastructure**: IIJA (Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act) driving demand for civil/structural/transportation engineers through 2030
- **EV / Clean Energy**: Electrical, power electronics, and battery engineers in acute shortage
- **Semiconductor / Advanced Manufacturing**: CHIPS Act funding triggering fab expansions; process, quality, and equipment engineers needed
- **Defense**: Continued DoD spending; mechanical, electrical, and systems engineers with clearance command premium

**Regional notes:**
- Texas, California, Ohio: heavy manufacturing and energy sector concentration
- Southeast (NC, SC, TN, AL): automotive and advanced manufacturing expansion
- Northeast (NY, PA, NJ): infrastructure, civil, and construction engineering
- Pacific Northwest: aerospace (Boeing supply chain) and tech-adjacent engineering

## Engineering Rate Context

Refer to `references/proposals-rates.md` for full rate card. Engineering-specific notes:
- PE-licensed engineers carry a 15–20% premium over unlicensed at same experience level
- Defense/cleared roles: add $20–$40/hr to standard market rate
- CAD-only roles (drafters) are below engineer rates — ensure the JD is clear on what's needed
- EV/battery and semiconductor: active premium of 20–30% above standard electrical engineering rates
- Project managers (PMP): premium over pure engineering; closer to senior engineering rates even at fewer years of experience
