## Sales Navigator — BD Search Strategies

### Building a Target Account List

**Account Filters (prioritize):**
- Industry: Hospital & Health Care, Staffing & Recruiting (competitors to learn from), Information Technology, Engineering, Financial Services
- Company headcount: 100–10,000 (too small = no staffing budget; too large = complex procurement)
- Growth: select "Growing" — companies with headcount growth hire more
- Geography: target metro areas or states aligned to your active candidate pipelines

**Lead Filters (decision-maker contacts):**
- Title keywords: "VP Engineering," "Director of Talent," "Chief Nursing Officer," "HR Business Partner," "Director of Operations"
- Seniority: Director, VP, C-suite (avoid Manager and below for first contact)
- Change in leadership (past 90 days): new leaders often audit vendors and change staffing partners — high-signal trigger

### Saved Searches & Alerts

Set up saved searches for:
1. "New decision-makers at target accounts" (senior hires + leadership changes filter)
2. "Growing companies in [domain] in [target metro]" (headcount growth filter)
3. "Companies posting [role type] jobs on LinkedIn" (job posting activity alert)

Check saved search alerts every Monday morning — they surface fresh triggers before the competition does.

### Account Mapping

For any high-priority target account, map three layers:
1. **Champion**: The person who benefits from the placement (Hiring Manager, Dept Head)
2. **Economic buyer**: The person who approves the vendor relationship (VP/Director of HR, CPO, CFO)
3. **Blocker**: Procurement, existing MSP, or vendor manager who controls access

Build relationships at all three levels simultaneously. Champions can pull you in even when procurement is slow.

## InMail Best Practices

### The HIS InMail Formula

```
Line 1: Personalized observation (not a compliment — an insight)
Line 2: What Hire'in does — one sentence, outcome-focused
Lines 3–4: 2 proof points relevant to their likely pain
Line 5: One low-commitment ask
Signature: Name + Hire'in Solutions + hire-in.com
```

### InMail Length

- **150 words max** — people read InMail on mobile; if it doesn't fit on one screen, it won't get read
- Never attach files or include links in a first InMail (triggers spam signals, reduces open rate)
- Use line breaks generously — walls of text get skipped

### InMail Timing

- **Best days**: Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday
- **Best times**: 7:30–9:00 AM (commute/start of day) or 12:00–1:30 PM (lunch)
- **Avoid**: Friday afternoon, Monday morning, and any major holiday week

### InMail Subject Lines (A/B test these)

- `Quick question about your [role type] pipeline`
- `[Company name] + Hire'in Solutions — 24-hr candidates`
- `[Mutual connection] suggested I reach out`
- `Your [job title] posting — we have candidates ready`
- `[Domain] staffing — thought this was relevant`

### InMail Personalization by Profile Signal

| What you see on their profile | Personalization angle |
|---|---|
| They posted about hiring challenges | "Saw your post about [challenge] — this is exactly the problem we solve" |
| Recent job change (< 6 months) | "Congrats on the new role — a lot of new leaders we work with use the first 90 days to audit their vendor stack" |
| They follow staffing/HR thought leaders | "You clearly stay on top of the talent market — curious what you're seeing in your hiring pipeline" |
| They share company news about growth | "Congrats on the [growth milestone] — growth at that pace usually means hiring gets harder fast" |
| They've engaged with a competitor's content | Don't mention the competitor — just position HIS strengths directly |

## Connection Request Strategy

### Who to Connect With

Priority order:
1. Warm introductions (mutual connection makes the request; 3–5× higher acceptance)
2. Event attendees (after a virtual or in-person event you've both attended)
3. People who engaged with your content (likes, comments)
4. Cold targets with a strong personalization reason

### Connection Request Message (300 char limit)

Always include a message. Blank requests from BD accounts get ignored.

**Template:**
```
Hi [Name] — I work with [domain] hiring leaders at companies like yours. 
Hire'in Solutions places pre-vetted candidates in 24 hrs via AI matching. 
Would love to connect and share what we're seeing in the [domain] market.
```

**Variant for mutual connection:**
```
Hi [Name] — [Mutual name] suggested I reach out. 
We help [domain] hiring teams fill hard roles faster with AI-powered sourcing. 
Would love to connect.
```

### Follow-Up After Accepted Connection

Wait 48 hours after acceptance, then send ONE follow-up message (not a pitch — a value add):

```
Hi [Name], thanks for connecting. 

I put together a short breakdown of what we're seeing in the [domain] 
talent market right now — happy to share it if useful. 

Alternatively, if you have a role that's been hard to fill, I'd love to 
hear about it — no pitch, just a conversation.

[Name] | Hire'in Solutions
```

Do not send a second follow-up message on LinkedIn without a new trigger (their post, a news event, etc.). LinkedIn limits and algorithmic suppression punish sequences of identical or near-identical messages.

## Buyer-Intent Signals (What to Watch For)

These LinkedIn behaviors signal a prospect is actively evaluating staffing help:

| Signal | What it means |
|---|---|
| Posting about open roles on their personal feed | Urgency — they're doing the recruiter's job themselves |
| Commenting on staffing/recruitment posts | Awareness of the market |
| Following competitor staffing firms | Shopping for vendors |
| Engaging with content about time-to-hire, cost-per-hire | They feel the pain metrics |
| Sharing a job posting with "who knows a great [role]?" | Struggling to source |
| Liking posts about "HR burnout" or "talent shortage" | Internal capacity stretched |
| Recent company headcount growth shown on LinkedIn | Hiring is accelerating |

Set up Sales Navigator alerts on high-priority accounts so you see these signals in near real-time.

## Content Strategy for Warm Lead Generation

Posting 2–3 times per week on LinkedIn as a HIS BD rep builds inbound interest. Use this content mix:

| Content type | Frequency | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Market insight (domain-specific trend) | 2×/month | Short text post, 150–200 words |
| Client success story (anonymized) | 1×/month | Story format with outcome metric |
| Candidate spotlight (anonymized) | 1×/month | "We have a [role] looking for their next opportunity" |
| Thought leadership (BD/hiring observation) | 1×/month | 300–500 word essay |
| Poll or question (engagement bait) | 1×/month | "What's your biggest hiring challenge right now?" |

## LinkedIn Etiquette Rules (Violations = Account Risk)

- **No spam sequences**: More than 3 messages without a reply = stop. Reported as spam = account restriction.
- **No scraping**: Don't export LinkedIn data in bulk using third-party tools not authorized by LinkedIn.
- **No fake personalization**: "I noticed your profile showed up in my search" is spam. Use real signals.
- **InMail credits are finite**: Don't waste credits on low-priority contacts. Save for decision-makers.
- **Don't connect-and-immediately-pitch**: This is the #1 reason people disconnect and mark as spam. Always warm up first.
