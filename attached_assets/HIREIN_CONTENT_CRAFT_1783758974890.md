# HIRE'IN CONTENT CRAFT — THE CREATIVE ENGINE
*How to write, not just what to write. This file makes the difference between competent and unmissable. Load with the other knowledge files.*

**How the AI must use this file:** Match the exemplars' quality bar, not their facts — every exemplar's Hire'in-specific claims are placeholders requiring real proof cards. Hooks and archetypes are starting frames, not fill-in templates; if the output feels like a template was filled in, rewrite it.

---

## 1. HOOK ENGINEERING (the first line decides everything)

Generate 3 hook options for every social piece using DIFFERENT archetypes below, label each, recommend one. A hook earns its place by creating a specific open question in the target reader's mind — not by being loud.

**The archetypes:**
1. **The mechanism reveal** — name the hidden cause of a familiar pain. *"Your OR req isn't stuck. Your titers are."*
2. **The insider contrast** — what amateurs do vs. what operators do. *"Weak agencies send you resumes. Strong ones send you evidence."*
3. **The expensive mistake** — a specific, costed error the reader is probably making. *"That 3-day delay returning interview feedback just cost you your top candidate — she had two other offers by Thursday."*
4. **The unasked question** — the question the reader should be asking but isn't. *"Nobody asks their staffing partner this one question. It predicts everything."*
5. **The counter-intuitive number/observation** — must be supportable or clearly framed as pattern-from-experience, never invented stats.
6. **The reader's inner monologue** — say what they're privately thinking. *"You're not 'behind on credentialing.' You were given a checklist designed for someone who's done this five times."* (H2 gold: validation before instruction)
7. **The stakes flip** — reframe who bears the risk. *"A bad submittal doesn't cost the agency anything. It costs you a week."*
8. **The specific scene** — drop the reader into a moment. *"Day 12 of a 13-week contract. Your recruiter hasn't mentioned extension. Here's what that silence usually means."*

**Hook rules:** under 12 words when possible for X/IG, under 20 for LinkedIn · no questions that answer themselves · no "Here's why" as the whole hook · never promise more than the body delivers · the second line must pay off the first, immediately.

## 2. STAFFING CONTENT ARCHETYPES (proven shapes, rotate deliberately)

1. **The Mechanism Explainer** — why a familiar problem actually happens, chokepoint by chokepoint. (Best: H1/I1, P1. The single strongest credibility format.)
2. **The Red-Flag Checklist** — signs of a bad contract/agency/req/candidate process. (Best: H2/I2, P2. The most shareable candidate format — nurses forward these.)
3. **The Question Set** — exact questions to ask (a recruiter, a staffing partner, in an intake call, in an interview). Concrete enough to screenshot.
4. **The Myth Autopsy** — take one belief ("more submittals = better odds") and dissect why it fails, with the mechanism.
5. **The Translation** — decode insider language for candidates ("what 'fast-paced environment' actually means in a req") or candidate language for employers ("what it means when your best applicants ghost after the second interview").
6. **The Timeline** — what happens between X and Y, step by step with realistic durations (offer→first shift, req→submittal, application→interview). Kills anxiety; builds trust.
7. **The Two Resumes / Two Reqs** — side-by-side of weak vs. strong (tool-list resume vs. ownership resume; vague req vs. calibrated req). Show, never just tell.
8. **The Contrarian Take** — a defensible position against industry consensus. Must be genuinely arguable and end with reasoning, not attitude. (Best: LinkedIn/X, I1/H1.)
9. **The Behind-the-Decision** — how a real decision gets made (how a recruiter picks 3 of 40 applicants; how a facility ranks submittals). Requires proof cards or clear pattern-framing.
10. **The Job Spotlight** — one open req told as an opportunity story, not a poster: who thrives in it, what's genuinely interesting, honest trade-offs. (P3, from Current Jobs file only.)
11. **The Micro Case** — one anonymized situation → intervention → outcome. HIGH RISK: requires proof card and privacy clearance; otherwise frame as composite pattern and say so.
12. **The Season Opener** — a seasonal moment (KB §8) + what to do about it now, before the crunch.

**Rotation rule:** never the same archetype twice in a row on the same platform. Track archetype in the working title.

## 3. WRITING CRAFT RULES (voice mechanics)

- **Specificity is the strategy.** Every piece needs at least two details a generalist couldn't write: a named chokepoint, a real duration, an exact question, a scene. "Credentialing takes time" is filler; "your ACLS card expires mid-assignment and nobody caught it at submission" is content.
- **Second person, active voice, present tense** wherever possible. The reader is in the content, not observing it.
- **One idea per paragraph; paragraphs of 1–3 sentences on social.** White space is pacing.
- **Earn every adjective.** Cut "very," "truly," "incredibly," "seamless," "robust." If a claim needs an intensifier, it needs evidence instead.
- **Numbers beat adjectives; scenes beat numbers.** Best: a scene with a number in it.
- **End with weight, not summary.** The last line before the CTA should be the takeaway said sharply — never "In conclusion" energy, never restating the hook.
- **Respect trumps cleverness for H2/I2.** Candidates live these problems; a too-clever tone about their stress reads as mockery. Warm, direct, zero condescension.
- **For H1/I1, peer-to-peer posture.** Write as an operator talking to an operator — never "let us educate you," never explaining terms they use daily.

## 4. BANNED — AI-SLOP AND STAFFING CLICHÉS (automatic rewrite)

Phrases: "in today's fast-paced world" · "the landscape of" · "game-changer" · "unlock/unleash" · "delve/dive into" · "navigate the complexities" · "it's important to note" · "at the end of the day" · "seamless/streamlined" (unless describing a specific mechanism) · "war for talent" · "people are our greatest asset" · "we go above and beyond" · "passionate about connecting people" · "top talent" as a noun · "dream job" · "rockstar/ninja/guru" · "work hard, play hard".
Patterns: opening with a definition · opening with "Are you struggling with…" · three-item rhetorical lists as a tic · "It's not just X, it's Y" more than once per month · exclamation points in B2B content · emoji as bullet points on LinkedIn (one or two inline emoji max, zero for H1/I1 unless the author's personal style) · hashtag piles (≤3 LinkedIn, ≤5 Instagram, ≤2 X, and only ones a human would search).

## 5. PLATFORM CRAFT SPECIFICS

**LinkedIn text post:** 150–300 words sweet spot. First 2 lines show before "…see more" — the hook AND its payoff must fit there. Line breaks every 1–2 sentences. One CTA, phrased as an invitation not a demand. Comment-bait questions only if you genuinely want the answers.

**Instagram carousel:** 7–10 slides. Cover: ≤8 words + visual promise of value. Slides: ≤25 words each, one idea, big type direction. Slide 2 must deliver value immediately (readers bail if slide 2 is throat-clearing). Final slide: recap + CTA. Caption: standalone value (150–200 words) — assume the carousel isn't opened. Alt text for every slide.

**Reel script:** 30–45 seconds. Hook spoken AND on-screen in first 3 seconds. Script column + on-screen text column + b-roll/visual column. One takeaway only. Talking-head-friendly: write how the presenter actually talks, contractions and all. CTA verbal + text overlay. Caption written separately.

**X single:** one idea, compressed until it hurts, then one more pass. No thread disguised as a post.
**X thread:** 5–9 posts. Post 1 = hook + promise of the payoff. Each post stands alone if screenshotted. Last post = sharp close + soft CTA. Never number posts "1/9" style unless genuinely long.

**Facebook (H2 community mode):** the screenshot-share test governs everything — practical, complete-in-itself, zero brand ego. Longer text fine; front-load the useful part.

**Insights article:** 800–1400 words. H2s every 200–300 words, written as reader questions where natural. One framework/checklist/table minimum. Examples in every major section. Meta-level SEO comes from genuinely answering the question, not keyword placement.

## 7. SELF-EDIT PASS (run before validation, every time)

1. Would the target reader stop scrolling at line one — and does line two pay it off?
2. Are there ≥2 details a generalist couldn't have written?
3. Read it aloud in your head — does any sentence sound like a press release? Rewrite it.
4. Scan against the banned list. Zero tolerance.
5. Does the ending land with weight, or does it deflate into summary?
6. Screenshot test (H2/I2 social): would a professional forward this to a peer?
7. Peer test (H1/I1): would an operator nod, or smell marketing?
