## SMART goals and development plan templates  
CIPD notes objectives are most effective when they focus on **specific outcomes** and are “SMART,” but for complex work it can be better to focus on **learning outcomes** rather than rigid targets. citeturn3view1  In a startup TA environment, many “outcomes” (fills) may be volume-dependent, so each role gets (a) outcome goals when there is hiring volume, and (b) learning/outcome surrogates (playbooks, simulations, conversion metrics) when volume is low. Where collaboration is critical, CIPD also notes team-based goals can make sense. citeturn3view1turn1view0  

### Development plan template for Hire’in  
Implement as a reusable form + workflow (employee + manager co-own). Keep it lightweight: one screen for “Plan,” one for “Check-in updates.”

**Development Plan fields (minimum):**  
- Role, Team (Healthcare / IT), Manager  
- Target track (from questionnaire) + secondary track  
- Competency targets (pick 3–5 competencies; current level and target level)  
- Goals (SMART + learning outcome) with due date and success metric  
- Learning activities (microlearning modules, shadowing, project, certification) with effort estimate  
- Evidence artifacts (links, notes, uploads)  
- Check-in cadence + next review date  
- Manager commitments (what support the manager will provide)

**Progress review form (monthly):**  
- Progress status (On track / At risk / Blocked)  
- New evidence added (auto-list)  
- Metric deltas (auto from dashboard)  
- What was learned (employee text)  
- What will change next month (employee + manager agreement)  
- Updated next actions and dates

## Curriculum, tracks, and questionnaire mapping  
CIPD’s performance-management framing explicitly connects objectives, feedback, learning and development, and regular discussions, which supports a “professional-development–focused” review experience inside Hire’in. citeturn1view0turn3view1  For delivery, CIPD research on digital learning highlights the value of **bite-sized, modular learning** (including micro-qualifications/credits) and skills frameworks that can stack into recognized journeys—helpful for cost control and incremental capability building. citeturn7view2  A lightweight portal implementation should therefore support: (a) microlearning links, (b) practice tasks, (c) evidence capture, and (d) manager coaching rituals.

### Recommended development tracks  
Implement tracks as tags used by: questionnaire results, resource recommendations, and development plan templates.

- **Track A: Healthcare Domain & Compliance** (Healthcare TA, Asst. Manager Healthcare)  
- **Track B: IT/Tech Domain Literacy** (IT TA, Asst. Manager IT)  
- **Track C: Sourcing Mastery (Boolean/OSINT + channel quality)** (Both TA teams) citeturn17view1turn20view0  
- **Track D: Stakeholder Partnering & Candidate Experience** (Both TA teams + leaders)  
- **Track E: Recruiting Analytics & Funnel Optimization** (Both TA + Delivery Manager) citeturn17view0turn7view4  
- **Track F: Coaching & TA Leadership** (Assistant Managers + Delivery Manager) citeturn3view0turn1view2  

### Questionnaire-to-track mapping rules (simple, explainable, cheap to run)  
Use a transparent scoring system (avoid “black box” in MVP). Store results: `primary_track`, `secondary_track`, `recommended_resources[]`.

**Inputs (examples):** strengths (multi-select), interests (single-select), industry affinity (multi-select), learning style (single-select).  
**Rules (example mapping):**  
- If “Healthcare / mission-driven” interest selected → +3 Track A.  
- If “Technical recruiting / fast-paced tech projects” selected → +3 Track B and +2 Track C.  
- If “Analytical / attention to detail” selected → +2 Track E.  
- If “Communication / empathy” selected → +2 Track D and +1 Track F.  
- Learning style affects delivery:  
  - Visual → recommend short videos + diagrams;  
  - Kinesthetic → recommend Replit demos + on-the-job projects;  
  - Reading/Writing → recommend playbooks and templates.

**Output in Hire’in (MVP):** “Primary track,” “Secondary track,” and “Next 2 micro-lessons,” plus “Suggested on-the-job project.”

### Microlearning modules and assessment methods  
Design microlearning as “bite-sized” modules that stack into journeys; this aligns with CIPD’s digital learning findings around modular, bite-sized learning journeys and skills frameworks. citeturn7view2  Keep modules 8–15 minutes and require a “work artifact” to reduce wasted training. Each module should end with (a) a quiz question set and (b) a practical artifact stored in the Hire’in plan.

**MVP microlearning set (12 modules):**  
- TA Foundations: intake excellence, structured screening notes, candidate messaging, offer closing basics, inclusion basics.  
- Healthcare TA: medical terminology, licensure/credentialing cues, shift/role realities.  
- IT TA: software roles vocabulary, cloud fundamentals, technical sourcing basics.  
- Analytics: time-to-hire definition and use, funnel conversion analysis, source quality tracking. citeturn17view0turn17view1  

**Assessment methods (cheap, high signal):**  
- Short quizzes (5–8 questions), pass threshold ≥80%  
- Artifact reviews (manager rubric score)  
- Shadowing + debrief checklist  
- “Simulated requisition” case exercises (especially when hiring volume is low)

### Sample evaluation rubrics (manager-friendly, low overhead)  
CIPD’s people-performance evidence review highlights that both subjective and objective measures have limitations and that using both helps achieve a fuller assessment. citeturn13view1  Implement rubrics as structured subjective measures, alongside your objective funnel metrics.

**Rubric: Intake meeting quality (scale 1–4)**  
| Level | Observable behaviors (evidence) |
|---|---|
| 1 – Needs support | Intake notes incomplete; success criteria unclear; timelines not agreed; repeated rework. |
| 2 – Working | Captures must-haves; some ambiguity remains; interview plan partially defined. |
| 3 – Proficient | Clear must/nice; evaluation plan and timeline aligned; risk areas identified. |
| 4 – Expert | Adds market insights; proposes trade-offs; aligns stakeholders; anticipates blockers. |

**Rubric: Structured screening note quality (scale 1–4)**  
- Evidence includes: credential/tech signals captured; motivation/constraints recorded; risk flags; next-step recommendation and rationale.

### Risks, mitigation, and adoption tactics for a cash-constrained startup  
- **Risk: “Training is extra work” → low adoption.** Mitigate by using bite-sized modules and tying learning to real work artifacts and check-ins. citeturn7view2turn1view0  
- **Risk: Metrics create fear or gaming.** Mitigate by framing metrics as health indicators and improvement signals, consistent with data-driven recruiting guidance. citeturn17view0  
- **Risk: Over-investing in expensive certifications early.** Mitigate by sequencing: free/low-cost learning first; paid certifications only for role multipliers (assistant managers) when impact is clear. citeturn13view8turn14view3turn14view4turn13view6  
- **Risk: Bias and inclusion regress under speed pressure.** Mitigate by embedding inclusive recruitment templates and monitoring data for bias sources, aligned with CIPD evidence-based guidance. citeturn13view2  
- **Risk: Coaching quality varies by manager.** Mitigate with standardized prompts, rubrics, and lightweight QA audits; coaching is a skilled activity and should be supported. citeturn3view0
