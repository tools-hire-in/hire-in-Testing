What is potentially differentiated

Your strongest product insight is:

Most HR systems record employee information. Your system can govern whether managers are executing the processes that protect performance, compliance, employee fairness, and business results.

That distinction matters.

A conventional system tells the CEO:

Your proposed system should tell the CEO:

Which goals are at risk, why, and what action is overdue?

Which department repeatedly misses check-ins?
What needs executive intervention this week?

That is a much stronger product thesis.

Entity recommendation

Because you intend to raise Friends and Family capital and potentially institutional capital, the cleanest structure is generally a dedicated U.S. Delaware C-corporation, not a software division of the staffing company.

YC’s post-money SAFE documents are specifically structured for early U.S. startup financing and make the percentage sold more transparent. Any securities raise still requires legal compliance: under Rule 506(b), general solicitation is restricted, non-accredited participation creates additional disclosure obligations, and a Form D notice is generally required within 15 days after the first sale.

This is structuring guidance, not legal or tax advice; startup counsel should implement the final arrangement.

Important compliance warning

The historical architecture document describes the system as “HIPAA-compliant,” but encryption, MFA, and audit logging by themselves do not establish HIPAA compliance. HHS describes compliance as an ongoing program involving administrative, physical, and technical safeguards, risk analysis, security measures, policies, documentation, and periodic evaluation.

Therefore:

Do not market the product as HIPAA-compliant without a defined legal scope and formal assessment.
Do not claim SOC 2 certification before an actual examination.
Market the current controls factually: encryption, MFA, RBAC, audit trails, and secure data handling.
