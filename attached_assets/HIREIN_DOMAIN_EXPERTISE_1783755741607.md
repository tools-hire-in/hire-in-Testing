## 1. HEALTHCARE STAFFING — SPEAK LIKE AN INSIDER

### Employment models (never blur these — each has a different reader)
- **Travel nursing / travel allied:** typically 13-week contracts (8–26 possible), housing stipend or agency housing, often crisis or seasonal demand. Reader cares about: pay package transparency (taxable base vs. stipends), extension likelihood, floating requirements, first-day expectations.
- **Per diem / PRN:** shift-by-shift, local, no guaranteed hours. Reader cares about: shift availability, cancellation policies, self-scheduling, facility variety.
- **Local contract:** contract terms without travel stipends; commuter rules matter.
- **Permanent placement (direct hire):** agency recruits, facility employs. Reader cares about: fit, culture, growth — not pay packages.
- **Locum tenens:** physician/APP coverage model — different credentialing depth, malpractice coverage questions, privileging timelines.
- **Interim leadership:** nurse managers, directors — a credibility-sensitive niche.

### Role & specialty vocabulary (use the real names)
RN specialties: ICU/CCU, ER/ED, OR (circulator vs. scrub), PACU, L&D, NICU (levels II–IV), Med-Surg, Tele/PCU (stepdown), Cath Lab, Oncology, Psych, Case Management, OR "first assist" (RNFA). Allied: Surgical Tech (CST), Rad Tech (ARRT registries by modality — CT, MRI, IR, Mammo), Respiratory Therapist (RRT vs. CRT), PT/OT/SLP, MLT/MLS lab roles, Sterile Processing (SPD). Advanced practice: NP (specialty certifications), PA, CRNA. Non-clinical: HIM/coders (CCS, CPC), credentialing specialists, patient access.

### Credentialing pipeline (the real mechanics behind "credentialing friction")
- **Licensure:** state RN license; **NLC (Nurse Licensure Compact)** multistate privilege vs. single-state license — and that compact privilege follows the nurse's primary state of residence. Never assert which states are compact members without checking; membership changes.
- **Certifications:** BLS/ACLS/PALS/NRP (typically AHA), specialty certs (CCRN, CEN, CNOR, TNCC).
- **Primary source verification (PSV):** verifying license/education directly with the issuing body — the standard hospitals and MSPs expect.
- **Typical file components:** license verification, certifications, immunization/titers, TB screening (PPD/QuantiFERON), fit testing, physical, drug screen, background check (often 7-year), OIG/SAM exclusion checks, skills checklists, specialty competency exams, references (commonly 2 supervisory within recent period), fingerprinting where required.
- **The Joint Commission Health Care Staffing Services (HCSS) certification** exists for staffing firms — it's a real differentiator agencies can hold (only claim it for Hire'in with proof).
- Why delays actually happen: expired documents at submission, titers requiring lab turnaround, state license processing queues, facility-specific modules/tests, references unresponsive, drug screen scheduling. Content that names these specific chokepoints reads as insider.

### Facility-side vocabulary
Bill rate vs. pay rate, VMS-listed vs. direct relationships, rate cards, float requirements, guaranteed hours vs. call-off allowances, block scheduling, first-day paperwork, unit orientation length, clinical ladder, nurse-to-patient ratios, Magnet designation, EMR requirements (Epic/Cerner-Oracle Health/Meditech experience is a real screening line-item).

## 2. IT & ENGINEERING STAFFING — SPEAK LIKE AN INSIDER

### Engagement models (readers self-identify by these instantly)
- **W-2 contract:** agency employs the consultant; simplest compliance for clients.
- **Corp-to-Corp (C2C):** consultant's own entity contracts with agency; common in senior/immigrant-visa-heavy markets; carries co-employment and compliance considerations clients care about.
- **1099:** independent contractor — misclassification risk is a real client concern; specialists talk about it plainly.
- **Contract-to-hire (right-to-hire):** conversion terms, conversion fees, typical 3–6 month evaluation windows.
- **Direct hire / retained vs. contingency search:** different fee models, different urgency signals.
- **SOW / project-based delivery:** outcomes-based scope vs. staff augmentation — the distinction MSPs police heavily.

### The vocabulary of screening signal (this is where I1 decides if you're real)
- **Keyword match vs. evidence of fit:** a resume saying "Kubernetes" vs. evidence the person *owned* cluster upgrades in production, on-call rotations, incident response.
- Real screening signals specialists probe: system ownership vs. contribution, production vs. sandbox experience, scale handled (users, requests, data volume), on-call/incident exposure, architectural decision involvement, regulated-environment exposure (HIPAA, SOC 2, PCI, FedRAMP, ITAR for defense-adjacent engineering).
- **Rate math clients respect:** bill rate = pay rate + burden (employer taxes, benefits, insurance) + margin. Content that acknowledges this openly builds trust; content that pretends margin doesn't exist reads as evasive.
- Submission quality vocabulary: submittal-to-interview ratio, interview-to-offer ratio, time-to-submit, right-to-represent (RTR) — and the candidate-side frustration of being submitted without consent, which is a trust topic candidates respond to strongly.

### Role vocabulary that signals depth
Distinguish: DevOps vs. SRE vs. Platform Engineering; Data Engineer vs. Data Scientist vs. Analytics Engineer; QA manual vs. SDET; embedded/firmware vs. application engineering; controls engineers (PLC/SCADA) vs. software; civil/structural/mechanical PE licensure where it applies (never assert state-specific PE requirements without proof).

## 3. MSP / VMS ECOSYSTEM (both audiences respect this fluency)

- **MSP (Managed Service Provider):** manages a client's entire contingent workforce program; agencies are suppliers within it.
- **VMS (Vendor Management System):** the software layer (e.g., platforms in the Fieldglass/Beeline/Wand category — name categories, not client relationships) where reqs, submittals, and timesheets live.
- Supplier-side realities specialists can speak to: req distribution tiers, response-time SLAs, scorecards, rate-card ceilings, submittal limits per req, program compliance audits, prime vendor vs. subcontracting arrangements ("sub-vendor" chains and the payment-terms pain that comes with them).
- Direct client vs. MSP-channel dynamics: speed and relationship depth vs. volume and structure. This trade-off is legitimate content territory.

## 3B. CHANNEL REALITY FOR H2 — FACEBOOK COMMUNITIES ARE NOT A FALLBACK

The platform rules correctly treat Facebook as "selective" for brand posting. But for healthcare professionals — travel nurses especially — **Facebook groups are the most active professional community that exists**: large travel-nurse networks, specialty nursing groups, and regional per-diem communities are where pay-package questions, agency reputations, facility reviews, and contract red-flags are actually discussed peer-to-peer.

Implications for content:
- H2 Facebook content should be written for a **community-conversation context**, not a brand-broadcast context: practical, screenshot-shareable, zero sales tone. The test: "would a nurse repost this into a group to help a peer?"
- Strong H2 Facebook formats: contract red-flag checklists, credential-prep timelines, "questions to ask a recruiter" lists, pay-package literacy explainers (taxable base vs. stipends).
- Agencies get discussed in these groups whether they participate or not — candidate-respect topics (RTR consent, transparent pay packages, responsive communication) are the content that earns organic mentions.
- Never write content directing employees of any kind to bypass or violate group rules; the play is making content worth sharing, not infiltrating communities.

## 4. RECRUITING OPERATIONS VOCABULARY (cross-domain)

Intake call quality (the difference between a req and an actual calibration), must-have vs. nice-to-have separation, scorecards/rubrics, structured interviews, candidate experience metrics (response time, feedback loops, ghosting — candidates reward agencies who name it honestly), redeployment (keeping contractors billing between assignments — the strongest quiet signal of a healthy agency), fill rate vs. quality-of-fill, time-to-fill vs. time-to-productivity, backfill, falloff (placements that fail early — honest content acknowledges falloff exists and what reduces it).

## 5. HOW TO DEPLOY THIS VOCABULARY (writing rules)

1. **Specific beats general, once per piece minimum.** "Credentialing delays" → "a missing titer or an expired ACLS card discovered at submission." "Technical screening" → "we ask what they owned in production, not what's on the tool list."
2. **Never define insider terms for insider audiences** (H1/I1) — using them naturally IS the credibility signal. DO briefly define them for candidate audiences (H2/I2) when helpful — teaching the vocabulary is a candidate-trust play.
3. **Name the mechanism, not just the problem.** Anyone can say "hiring is slow." A specialist says *why*: reference lag, license queue, VMS submittal caps, interview-panel scheduling.
4. **Honest about trade-offs = specialist tone.** Margin exists. Falloff happens. MSP channels have rate ceilings. Naming these calmly is what separates operator content from marketing content.
5. **Never convert this fluency into a Hire'in capability claim.** "Strong agencies verify certifications before submission" = allowed (industry knowledge). "Hire'in verifies certifications before submission" = requires a Proof Card.
6. **State-specific licensure/credential rules are a hard [NEEDS_PROOF]** — always. Compact membership, PE requirements, scope-of-practice rules change and vary; never assert them from memory.
