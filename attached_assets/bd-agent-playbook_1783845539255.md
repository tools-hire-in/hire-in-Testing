# Hire'in Solutions Business Development and Client Communication Standards

**Version:** 2.2  
**Effective date:** July 12, 2026  
**Document owner:** CEO and Business Development Leadership  
**Knowledge owner:** Designated owner of approved positioning, proof points, and client-facing source materials  
**Applies to:** Business Development, Account Management, Delivery, Recruitment, Marketing, Operations, and Leadership  
**Review cycle:** Quarterly and whenever a material workflow, claim, domain, deck, or BD Agent behavior changes

**Operating principle:** One company. One standard. One professional client experience.

## 1. Purpose of This Standard

Every email, call, meeting, capability deck, proposal, candidate update, and follow-up sent on behalf of Hire'in Solutions shapes the client's perception of our professionalism, credibility, and delivery quality.

This standard establishes how Hire'in Solutions:

- selects and qualifies business development opportunities;
- positions its value by staffing domain and buyer type;
- creates and governs capability decks and client-facing materials;
- communicates with clients, MSPs, VMS teams, partners, and prospects;
- verifies and approves proof points and claims;
- uses the Hire'in BD Agent to support consistent execution; and
- maintains clear human ownership and approval before external communication.

The purpose is not to slow the team or discourage initiative. The purpose is to give the team an efficient and repeatable system so members can focus on the client's need, the opportunity, the strongest relevant proof, and the correct next action while the company's brand, language, quality, and governance remain consistent.

This document is the authoritative internal standard for business development and client communication. When the standard and an informal practice conflict, this standard controls unless leadership approves an exception in writing.

## 2. Core Business Development Principles

Hire'in Solutions will follow these principles in every opportunity:

- Start with the buyer's situation and decision, not with a generic description of Hire'in.
- Lead with no more than two or three relevant value arguments in a conversation or document.
- Use only proof points that have an approved source, owner, wording, and usage status.
- Separate verified facts from assumptions, recommendations, and future capabilities.
- Recommend the smallest practical next step that allows the client to evaluate our delivery.
- Prefer focused opportunities where our operating model and evidence are relevant.
- Do not pursue every requirement merely because it is available.
- Never allow AI-generated content, a draft deck, or an unverified claim to be shared externally without human review.

Hire'in does not compete by saying that it can fill every role, serve every market, or guarantee every outcome. We build credibility by demonstrating that we understand the requirement, ask the right questions, apply disciplined screening, communicate clearly, and take ownership of the next step.

## 3. Where Hire'in Wins

The team must use this section to decide which opportunities deserve priority. These win profiles are more important than a broad list of services.

### 3.1 Focused Permanent Healthcare Hiring

Hire'in is best positioned when a hospital, health system, clinic, outpatient organization, rehabilitation provider, diagnostics organization, or staffing partner needs focused support on permanent nursing, allied health, or clinical and non-clinical support roles.

**Strong fit signals:**

- The client has a defined group of priority roles, locations, or specialties.
- The buyer values relevant submissions more than raw candidate volume.
- The client will clarify must-haves, schedule, location, compensation, and credential expectations.
- The client wants support through interview coordination, offer activity, or onboarding follow-through.
- The engagement can begin with a pilot or a manageable group of requisitions.

**Why we can be relevant:**

- Role calibration before broad sourcing
- Targeted active and passive outreach
- Role-specific pre-screening
- Credential-aware review when required
- Submission-readiness quality control
- Clear coordination from intake through onboarding

### 3.2 Buyers Experiencing Submission Noise or Incomplete Candidate Packages

Hire'in is well positioned when the client's problem is not only candidate availability, but excessive irrelevant submissions, weak screening notes, incomplete candidate information, or repeated back-and-forth.

**Strong fit signals:**

- Hiring managers are spending too much time screening unsuitable resumes.
- Vendors are missing required information or submission formats.
- Candidate interest, availability, location, compensation, or credentials are not being confirmed consistently.
- The client wants fewer, better-aligned submissions.

**Primary value argument:** Hire'in applies structured pre-screening and submission-readiness review so the client receives more complete and relevant candidate information.

### 3.3 Structured MSP, VMS, and Partner-Led Programs

Hire'in can be competitive when the buyer values process discipline, documented status, submission completeness, responsiveness, and a clear escalation path.

**Strong fit signals:**

- The program has defined submission rules and templates.
- Candidate ownership and status documentation matter.
- The MSP or VMS team expects timely acknowledgment and follow-through.
- Priority roles or bottlenecks require clear escalation.
- The client values consistent notes and reporting.

Use this win profile only when the exact program requirements are understood and Hire'in can operate within them. Do not claim broad MSP or VMS performance without verified account evidence.

### 3.4 Focused Pilot Opportunities

Hire'in is likely to win when a client is willing to test performance on a defined set of roles, locations, or requisitions before considering a wider rollout.

A strong pilot has:

- clearly defined roles and locations;
- agreed must-haves and submission standards;
- an identified client and Hire'in owner;
- a communication cadence;
- two to four measurable success indicators; and
- a defined review point for expansion, adjustment, or closure.

The preferred pilot ask is not "send us everything." It is: "Let us demonstrate our process on a focused group of roles where quality, responsiveness, and communication can be measured."

### 3.5 Buyers Who Value Direct Access, Ownership, and Flexibility

Hire'in can compete against larger vendors when the buyer values access to decision-makers, faster escalation, adaptable delivery support, and a partner willing to work within the client's operating model.

This is a service-model advantage. It must not be presented as an unsupported claim that Hire'in is always faster, cheaper, or better than a larger competitor.

## 4. Where We Should Be Selective

The team must not treat every opportunity as equally attractive. Hire'in should qualify carefully or decline when:

- the buyer is selecting solely on the lowest rate and does not value quality or process;
- the program requires nationwide deployment scale that has not been verified;
- the client expects guaranteed placements, guaranteed turnaround, or guaranteed compliance;
- the opportunity requires certifications, accreditations, licenses, or program experience that Hire'in cannot substantiate;
- the role categories are outside demonstrated recruiting expertise;
- the client will not provide enough information to calibrate the requirement;
- the commercial terms create an unacceptable margin, payment, legal, or operational risk;
- the opportunity requires 24/7 surge coverage that has not been operationally established;
- the buyer expects large-volume travel, per diem, locum, or temporary healthcare delivery without verified infrastructure; or
- the opportunity would require use of unapproved client names, metrics, or performance claims to appear credible.

A decision not to pursue is not a failure. It protects team capacity and allows the company to focus on opportunities it can serve credibly.

## 5. Prioritized Value Strategy

Hire'in has four company-wide value pillars. A representative should normally lead with only the two or three most relevant pillars for the domain, buyer, and opportunity.

### 5.1 Company-Wide Value Pillars

1. **Relevant, submission-ready talent** - Candidates and recommendations should be aligned to the stated requirement, properly screened, and presented with the information needed for a decision.
2. **Responsive and disciplined delivery** - The team should act with urgency while maintaining quality, documentation, and realistic expectations.
3. **Domain- and credential-aware screening** - Screening should reflect the role, environment, technology, clinical scope, licenses, certifications, and client-specific conditions that matter.
4. **Clear ownership and operational visibility** - Clients should understand what is being worked on, what has changed, what is blocked, who owns the next action, and when the next update will occur.

### 5.2 Healthcare Value Priorities

Lead with these three, in order:

1. **Credential-aware, submission-ready candidates**
2. **Quality and relevance of submissions**
3. **Responsive coordination through interview and onboarding**

**Use when speaking with:** healthcare hiring managers, talent acquisition leaders, MSP/VMS program teams, clinical operations leaders, and healthcare staffing partners.

**Approved message direction:**

- We calibrate the role before broad sourcing.
- We confirm role-specific experience, interest, availability, schedule, location, and compensation alignment.
- We apply credential-aware checks based on the role and program requirements.
- We review candidate-package completeness before submission.
- We provide clear coordination and status through the hiring process.

**Do not lead with:** nationwide reach, 24/7 delivery, candidates in hours, guaranteed compliance, or named health-system experience unless the exact claim is approved in the Proof Point Registry.

### 5.3 IT Value Priorities

Lead with these three, in order:

1. **Accurate alignment to technical and business requirements**
2. **Speed to qualified and available candidates**
3. **Reliable communication and ownership throughout the process**

**Approved message direction:**

- We clarify required skills, practical experience, work model, location, authorization, compensation, and project context.
- We distinguish must-have qualifications from preferred qualifications.
- We present candidate fit, gaps, availability, and risks transparently.
- We maintain clear ownership and follow-through from submission to start.

**Proof requirement:** IT delivery claims must be tied to active or documented client, consultant, requisition, placement, or performance records. Internal financial assumptions are not external proof.

### 5.4 Engineering and General Professional Value Priorities

Lead with these three, in order:

1. **Role-specific screening and practical fit**
2. **Focused support for priority or difficult requirements**
3. **Clear accountability and communication**

Use conservative positioning until the company has approved case studies, delivery metrics, and proof points for each professional domain.

## 7. Client Communication Tone and Language

The Hire'in tone must be:

- professional and prepared;
- confident without being aggressive;
- warm and respectful;
- concise without being abrupt;
- consultative rather than transactional;
- specific rather than generic; and
- honest about what is known, unknown, or still being verified.

### 7.1 Language We Should Use

- "Based on the roles you are prioritizing, we recommend..."
- "To ensure we target the correct candidate profile, we would like to clarify..."
- "We reviewed the requirement and identified the following factors that may affect candidate availability..."
- "Our proposed first step is a focused pilot covering..."
- "Here is what has been completed, what remains open, and what we recommend next."
- "We do not yet have enough verified information to confirm that point. We will validate it before including it externally."

### 7.2 Language and Behaviors to Avoid

- "We can provide any resource anywhere."
- "We are the best staffing company."
- "We guarantee immediate placements."
- "Please give us requirements."
- Repeated follow-ups that add no new value
- Long introductions focused mainly on Hire'in
- Desperate, exaggerated, or overly promotional language
- Unsupported claims about clients, compliance, coverage, speed, results, certifications, or scale
- Internal shorthand, spelling errors, unfinished messages, or casual language in formal client communication

## 8. Standard Communication Framework

Most client communication should follow four parts.

### 8.1 Context

Acknowledge the specific requirement, conversation, meeting, issue, or decision. Demonstrate that the message is written for this client rather than copied from a generic template.

### 8.2 Relevance and Value

Connect the client's situation to no more than two or three relevant value pillars. Explain why the point matters to the buyer.

### 8.3 Evidence, Action, or Recommendation

State what has been reviewed, completed, identified, or recommended. Use only proof points with an allowed claim status.

### 8.4 Clear Next Step

End with one specific request, decision, meeting, clarification, or action. Avoid vague endings when a precise next step is possible.

## 9. Opportunity Qualification Standard

Before a capability discussion, proposal, pilot, or customized deck is prepared, the account owner must answer the following five questions:

1. What business or workforce problem is the client trying to solve?
2. Why is the requirement important now, and what is the cost or risk of delay?
3. What buyer, process, domain, role, location, volume, and delivery conditions must be satisfied?
4. Why is Hire'in relevant for this specific situation, and which approved proof points support that relevance?
5. What is the smallest practical next action that can advance or test the opportunity?

The BD Agent and account owner should produce one of four qualification verdicts:

- **Pursue:** Strong alignment with a defined win profile, credible proof, viable commercial conditions, and a clear next step.
- **Qualify further:** Potential alignment exists, but material information or proof is missing.
- **Nurture:** The account may be relevant later, but timing, access, or priority is insufficient now.
- **Do not pursue:** The opportunity conflicts with selectivity criteria, requires unsupported claims, or creates unacceptable delivery or commercial risk.

A customized deck should not be created merely because a prospect requested information. The team must first identify the decision the deck is intended to support.

## 10. AI-Led Market Intelligence, Client Positioning, and Opportunity Strategy

### 10.1 Virtual CBDO Copilot Role

The Hire'in BD Agent operates as a governed Virtual Chief Business Development Officer Copilot. It combines market research, account intelligence, buyer context, Hire'in's approved knowledge, and opportunity information to recommend how the company should qualify, position, pursue, and advance an opportunity.

The Agent is expected to provide strategic direction, not only generate messages or presentations. It should help the team answer:

- Is this opportunity attractive and relevant to Hire'in?
- Does it align with a situation where Hire'in is likely to win?
- Which client problem should lead the conversation?
- Which one primary and two supporting value arguments are most relevant?
- What proof is available, and what still requires verification?
- What risks or selectivity concerns could reduce the likelihood of success?
- What is the most practical next action?
- What should Hire'in avoid saying, promising, or emphasizing?

The Agent recommends and explains strategy. Authorized humans retain authority for final positioning, pursuit decisions, commercial commitments, and all external communication.

### 10.2 Market and Account Intelligence Inputs

The Agent may use:

- team-provided market research;
- public industry and workforce research;
- account and buyer research;
- discovery notes and client-provided information;
- job, requirement, location, volume, and rate information;
- MSP, VMS, procurement, and vendor-program information;
- delivery-team and candidate-market feedback;
- competitor observations;
- previous approved decks, proposals, emails, and meeting summaries;
- approved internal knowledge sources; and
- current account and opportunity records.

Information submitted by a team member is not automatically verified or approved.

### 10.3 Research Provenance and Reliability

Before using research in a strategic recommendation, the Agent should evaluate and preserve, when available:

- source and source type;
- publication, observation, or submission date;
- author or submitting user;
- domain and geography;
- account or opportunity association;
- reliability;
- verification status;
- confidentiality;
- whether the information is factual, interpretive, inferred, or speculative;
- whether it conflicts with approved company knowledge; and
- whether additional verification is required.

Use clear labels such as:

- Verified external source
- Client-provided information
- Approved internal knowledge
- Team observation
- Inference
- Requires verification
- Conflicting evidence
- Insufficient information

The Agent must not present an inference, observation, or unverified research item as a confirmed fact.

### 10.4 Client Positioning Recommendation

For each meaningful opportunity, the Agent should produce a structured Client Positioning Recommendation containing:

1. **Client situation**
   - client problem;
   - business impact or risk;
   - urgency;
   - buyer role and priorities;
   - opportunity stage.

2. **Recommended positioning**
   - one primary positioning statement;
   - one primary value pillar;
   - no more than two supporting value pillars;
   - recommended tone and urgency;
   - capabilities or messages that should not lead.

3. **Hire'in relevance**
   - matched Where-We-Win scenario;
   - supporting fit signals;
   - relevant Hire'in capabilities;
   - available proof points;
   - evidence gaps and required qualifications.

4. **Competitive strategy**
   - likely alternatives or incumbent approach, when supported by evidence;
   - where Hire'in can credibly differentiate;
   - where a larger or specialist competitor may be stronger;
   - whether to lead with specialization, submission quality, process discipline, direct access, ownership, flexibility, responsiveness, or a focused pilot;
   - whether competing mainly on price would weaken the opportunity.

5. **Recommended next action**
   - discovery meeting;
   - clarification request;
   - focused pilot;
   - approved capability deck;
   - market-feedback discussion;
   - leadership introduction;
   - initial candidate slate;
   - nurture;
   - leadership review; or
   - deprioritization.

The Agent must not invent competitor facts or claim superiority without evidence.

### 10.5 Opportunity Strategy Verdicts

The Agent may recommend:

- **Pursue:** Strong alignment with current capabilities, a defined win profile, credible proof, viable commercial conditions, and a clear next action.
- **Qualify further:** Potential alignment exists, but material information, access, evidence, or commercial clarity is missing.
- **Pilot recommended:** The opportunity is promising, but a limited role set, location, workstream, or evaluation period is the most credible entry point.
- **Nurture:** The account may be strategically relevant, but the timing, urgency, access, or current need is insufficient for active pursuit.
- **Leadership review required:** The opportunity involves a new market, significant commitment, strategic partnership, unusual commercial terms, material risk, or reliance on unverified claims.
- **Do not prioritize:** The opportunity has weak strategic or economic fit, conflicts with current capacity or evidence, or is unlikely to justify the cost of pursuit.

These are advisory recommendations. The Agent must not automatically reject, close, archive, or approve an opportunity.

### 10.6 Opportunity Assessment Factors

The Agent should consider:

- domain and service-model alignment;
- buyer access and decision influence;
- urgency and cost of delay;
- hiring volume and role difficulty;
- geography and work model;
- delivery capacity;
- availability of approved proof;
- rate, fee, margin, and commercial viability;
- procurement, MSP, VMS, or compliance demands;
- competitive intensity;
- willingness to begin with a focused pilot;
- strategic account value;
- cost of pursuit;
- risk of overcommitting; and
- unanswered questions.

The Agent should explain the most influential positive and negative factors rather than output only a score.

### 10.7 Where-We-Win and Selectivity Use

The five Where-We-Win profiles are strategic guidance. The Agent should identify:

- the matched scenario or lack of a strong match;
- the evidence supporting the match;
- missing information;
- recommended positioning because of the match; and
- the most relevant pilot or next action.

The selectivity guardrails must be equally visible. They should generate warnings and leadership-review recommendations when appropriate, but must not automatically create a no-bid decision.

### 10.8 Proof-Point Selection

The Agent may recommend proof points based on the domain, buyer role, client problem, opportunity stage, channel, audience, and documented restrictions.

Every surfaced material proof point should show:

- exact approved or proposed wording;
- status;
- source;
- qualifier or restriction; and
- review date, when available.

A human must confirm suitability before external use.

### 10.9 Human Decision Record

The system should preserve the Agent's recommendation separately from the final human decision.

Human decisions may include:

- Accepted
- Modified
- Escalated
- Deprioritized
- Pending review

The reviewer should be able to record rationale or modifications. The Agent's original recommendation must remain available for audit, evaluation, and learning.

### 10.10 Reuse Across Workflows

After a human accepts or modifies a positioning recommendation, the current strategy should be reused by:

- Pre-Call Brief;
- Discovery and Profile Building;
- Qualification;
- Deck Collaborator;
- Follow-Up Drafter;
- Meeting Summary;
- Pilot Proposal;
- Objection Response; and
- Manager Review.

A material new discovery may trigger a proposed revision, but the system must not silently replace the current human-reviewed positioning.

### 10.11 What the Agent Must Not Do

The BD Agent must not:

- approve a new company claim;
- automatically reject or close an opportunity;
- commit pricing, margins, timelines, SLAs, guarantees, or contractual terms;
- present unverified market research as confirmed;
- invent a client problem or buyer priority;
- claim superiority over a competitor without evidence;
- use prohibited or internal-only claims externally;
- automatically send messages, decks, or proposals;
- replace client discovery with assumptions; or
- represent an AI-generated strategy as final leadership approval.

### 10.12 Current-Phase Operating Principle

During the development and scale phase, the Agent should think like a disciplined CBDO while operating as a governed decision-support system.

AI recommends, explains, drafts, and identifies risk. Human leadership and authorized account owners approve, modify, and own the decision.

## 11. Capability Deck and Client-Facing Material Standards

Capability decks, proposals, and presentations are controlled assets.

### 10.1 Authorized Creation and Modification

- Only authorized users may create or modify capability decks.
- Delivery and recruitment teams may provide client context, requirements, market observations, operating details, and candidate or delivery inputs.
- Unapproved personal templates must not be used externally.
- An approved master deck may not be modified and shared externally without the required review.

### 10.2 Content Standards

- Open with the client's context, decision, or opportunity.
- Prioritize only the relevant capabilities and value arguments.
- Use approved proof points from the Proof Point Registry.
- Label or remove unsupported, restricted, expired, or superseded claims.
- Distinguish current capability from planned capability.
- End with a clear recommendation or next step.

### 10.3 Brand and Presentation Standards

- Use approved Hire'in naming, logo, colors, typography, layout, and footer.
- Keep slides concise, readable, and decision-oriented.
- Avoid random colors, inconsistent graphics, excessive text, copied competitor content, and client logos without authorization.
- Verify spelling, grammar, alignment, page numbering, contact information, and attachments.

### 10.4 Approval Workflow

Client-facing decks must follow:

1. Draft
2. Internal review
3. Pending approval
4. Approved for defined use
5. Shared externally
6. Superseded, expired, or retired when no longer current

Only an approved version from the central repository may be shared externally.

## 12. Proof Point and Claim Governance

The Proof Point Registry is the controlled list of statements that the team and the BD Agent may use.

A capability deck is a source document. It does not automatically make every statement inside it an approved proof point. Each material claim must have an approved wording, source, owner, status, and usage condition.

### 11.1 Claim Statuses

- **Approved:** Reviewed and available for human consideration within its documented audience, domain, and channel. Final human review remains required before external use.
- **Approved with qualification:** Available for human consideration only when the exact qualifier, limitation, audience, and usage condition are preserved.
- **Requires verification:** May be surfaced internally for review but must not be used externally until supporting evidence and wording are manually verified.
- **Internal only:** May inform internal planning and strategy but must not appear in client-facing material.
- **Prohibited:** Must not be presented as approved, inserted into client-facing content, or used externally.
- **Expired:** Was previously approved but is unavailable for external reuse until manually reviewed and reapproved.
- **Superseded:** Replaced by newer wording or evidence and must not be selected for new external content.

### 11.2 Required Registry Fields

Each proof point must include:

- Proof-point ID
- Domain and claim category
- Exact approved language
- Source document and source location
- Evidence owner
- Approval status
- Approved buyer types and channels
- Required qualifiers or restrictions
- Effective date
- Review or expiration date
- Superseded wording, when applicable
- Internal notes

### 11.3 Current-Phase Manual Governance Policy

During Hire'in's development, pilot, and early-scale phase, proof-point governance will remain manual and human-controlled. The registry organizes approved language, sources, restrictions, and review status, but it is not an autonomous approval engine.

The BD Agent may retrieve, organize, label, and recommend proof points. It may not approve its own claims, convert an internal assumption into external proof, remove a required qualifier, or determine final client positioning.

### 11.4 Human Review Requirement

Every client-facing claim, deck, proposal, email, recommendation, and follow-up created with assistance from the BD Agent must be reviewed by an authorized human before external use.

The reviewer must confirm:

- the proof point is relevant to the client, buyer, domain, and opportunity;
- the supporting source is accurate, current, and appropriate for the intended use;
- the approved wording and any required qualification are preserved;
- the statement does not overstate Hire'in's experience, capacity, coverage, results, compliance, or delivery capability;
- the final communication supports the current account strategy and relationship; and
- all required leadership, commercial, brand, or client-specific approvals have been obtained.

The BD Agent must never automatically send a client communication or externally distribute a deck.

### 11.5 MVP Registry Behavior

During the current phase, the Proof Point Registry should support disciplined human decision-making without forcing a fixed market position into every opportunity.

The system should:

- store proof points with their sources, statuses, restrictions, qualifiers, owners, and review dates;
- allow authorized users to search and filter the registry;
- surface potentially relevant proof points based on the client, buyer, domain, and opportunity;
- display the source, status, qualification, and restrictions beside every surfaced material claim;
- warn when a claim requires verification or additional leadership review;
- prevent prohibited, internal-only, expired, and superseded claims from being presented as approved external proof;
- record who created, updated, reviewed, or changed the status of a proof point; and
- keep all Agent-generated content subject to human editing and approval.

The Agent may recommend a proof point. The account owner or authorized reviewer decides whether it is suitable for the specific client and communication.

### 11.6 Authorization During the Current Phase

Proof-point responsibilities will use the existing role and feature-permission system. No new database role is required for this phase.

- **Super Admin:** May create, edit, classify, restrict, expire, supersede, and retire proof-point records.
- **CEO or designated business leadership:** Manually reviews and approves material external wording.
- **BD representatives and account owners:** May view and consider approved proof points but may not change approval status unless explicitly authorized.
- **Delivery, recruitment, and marketing:** May submit evidence, corrections, or proposed proof points for review.
- HR system access does not automatically provide authority over business development claims.
- During this phase, the CEO or a designated business leader may perform the operating responsibilities of the Knowledge Owner without creating a separate technical role.

### 11.7 Client-Specific Flexibility

The registry must not force the same value proposition or proof point into every opportunity. Before surfacing recommendations, the BD Agent should consider:

- client type and operating environment;
- buyer role and decision responsibility;
- staffing domain and role category;
- opportunity stage, urgency, and engagement model;
- the buyer's stated problem and desired outcome;
- relevant documented Hire'in experience and available evidence; and
- the most appropriate next action.

An authorized user may decide not to use an approved proof point when it is not relevant, persuasive, or appropriate for the client.

### 11.8 Where We Win and Selectivity Guidance

The five Where Hire'in Wins scenarios are strategic guidance and fit signals. They are not automatic qualification or rejection rules.

The BD Agent should identify possible alignment, explain why the opportunity may fit, surface discovery questions, identify missing information, and recommend a qualification verdict for human review.

The selectivity guardrails must be equally visible. During this phase, they should generate warnings and additional qualification questions rather than automatic no-bid decisions.

Only an authorized account owner or business leader may make the final pursue, nurture, deprioritize, or no-bid decision.

### 11.9 Master Decks and Proof Points

Master decks and the Proof Point Registry serve different but complementary purposes.

- **Master decks** provide approved narrative flow, slide structure, domain positioning, visual context, and standard company presentation language.
- **The Proof Point Registry** provides atomic claims, source references, status, usage restrictions, required qualifiers, and review history.

The BD Agent may use both to prepare a draft, but the existence of a statement in a master deck does not automatically make it approved proof. A human must review the final narrative and every material externally used claim.

### 11.10 Deferred Governance Features

The following features are intentionally deferred until Hire'in has more stable positioning, client evidence, usage data, and governance maturity:

- autonomous claim approval or publication;
- automatic opportunity rejection or no-bid decisions;
- multi-level or complex approval routing;
- a dedicated Knowledge Owner database role;
- automatic quarterly review workflows;
- automatic client-specific claim authorization;
- automatic external sharing or sending; and
- complex evidence-management and evidence-attachment workflows.

### 11.11 Product Principle

The BD Agent is a decision-support and quality-control tool. It helps the team work with greater consistency, preparation, and access to governed knowledge, but it does not replace human judgment, client discovery, account strategy, leadership approval, commercial decision-making, or final review of client-facing materials.

**During the development and scale phase, human control takes priority over automation.**

## 14. Approval, Documentation, and Version Control

- Approved materials must be stored in the designated central repository.
- File names must identify the document type, client or purpose when appropriate, and version or approval date.
- Only one current approved master version should be available for routine use.
- Superseded, expired, restricted, internal-only, and retired versions must be clearly marked or archived.
- Important client commitments and approvals must be recorded in the approved system.
- Verbal approval alone is insufficient for controlled materials.
- Pricing, margins, commercial exceptions, partnership terms, contract commitments, named-client references, and strategic claims require authorized leadership approval.

## 15. How the Hire'in BD Agent Enforces This Standard

The Hire'in BD Agent is the operating tool that helps the team apply this standard consistently. It does not replace discovery, judgment, ownership, or approval.

### 14.1 What the BD Agent Does

- Builds a pre-call brief using approved account and knowledge context
- Guides discovery and opportunity-profile completion
- Produces an advisory qualification verdict linked to the Where Hire'in Wins criteria for human review
- Selects the two or three most relevant value pillars by domain and buyer
- Surfaces potentially relevant proof points with their source, status, qualifiers, and restrictions for human selection
- Labels approved, qualified, unverified, internal-only, prohibited, expired, and superseded claims
- Recommends a next best action and an appropriate pilot ask
- Helps tailor approved capability decks without changing protected brand elements
- Drafts client communication using the approved tone and four-part framework
- Preserves version, approval, source, and audit information
- Provides context-sensitive access to this playbook

### 14.2 What the BD Agent Does Not Do

- It does not approve its own claims or source material.
- It does not make an unsupported statement valid by repeating it.
- It does not replace a representative's discovery or relationship judgment.
- It does not commit pricing, terms, guarantees, timelines, or legal obligations.
- It does not send external communication automatically.
- It does not remove mandatory qualifiers.
- It does not authorize use of a draft or expired deck.
- It does not treat financial projections as customer-facing evidence.

### 14.3 Required User Responsibilities

The user remains responsible for:

- entering accurate client and opportunity context;
- confirming the buyer and intended use;
- reviewing generated content;
- correcting assumptions or missing information;
- selecting the proper approved source;
- obtaining required approval; and
- ensuring the final external communication is appropriate for the relationship.

### 14.4 Product Enforcement Requirements

The BD Agent must:

- block prohibited claims from being presented as approved or inserted into client-facing drafts;
- clearly warn and require manual verification for claims marked Requires verification;
- allow only exact or qualified approved language where required;
- show the source and status of every material proof point;
- treat Where Hire'in Wins and selectivity guardrails as advisory fit signals requiring human review, not automatic pursue or rejection rules;
- prevent draft, expired, restricted, or superseded decks from being marked externally approved;
- record the human reviewer, approval decision, and version history when those actions occur;
- surface the relevant playbook section in the active workflow; and
- never auto-approve, auto-reject an opportunity, or auto-send a message or deck.

## 16. Team Rhythms and KPIs

### 15.1 Daily Habits

- Review upcoming client meetings and define the decision, questions, and next action.
- Complete or update the opportunity profile.
- Use approved proof points and current materials.
- Record material commitments and follow-ups.
- Escalate claims, pricing, or approval questions before external sharing.

### 15.2 Weekly Team Rhythm

- Review active opportunities against the Where Hire'in Wins criteria.
- Examine stalled opportunities and the quality of next actions.
- Review one or two examples of client communication or deck tailoring.
- Capture new objections, market feedback, and proof-point needs.
- Review unverified claims and expired content awaiting action.

### 15.3 Monthly Review

- Review conversion by opportunity stage and domain.
- Review proof-point use, approval exceptions, and content gaps.
- Review deck usage, approval cycle time, and outdated versions.
- Review whether wins and losses support or challenge the current win profiles.
- Update the playbook and agent configuration when evidence changes.

### 15.4 Nine Core KPIs

1. Percentage of active opportunities with a complete opportunity profile
2. Percentage of client meetings with a completed pre-call brief
3. Percentage of opportunities matched to an approved win profile
4. Percentage of generated external content using approved proof points only
5. Number of unverified or prohibited claims caught before external sharing
6. Deck approval cycle time
7. Client response or meeting-progression rate after tailored follow-up
8. Submission-to-interview conversion by domain and client
9. Pilot-to-expanded-engagement or revenue-attribution rate

### 15.5 Three Leading Indicators of Healthy Adoption

- Representatives consistently narrow the message to two or three relevant value arguments.
- Opportunity reviews include a specific qualification verdict and next action rather than general activity updates.
- The Proof Point Registry becomes more complete and accurate through real delivery evidence, not unsupported marketing language.

## 17. Pre-Send and Pre-Share Checklist

Before sending an important message or document, confirm:

- The recipient, company, buyer role, and purpose are correct.
- The message reflects the client's situation rather than a generic pitch.
- No more than two or three relevant value pillars are emphasized.
- The opportunity fits an approved win profile or the limitations are acknowledged.
- All material facts and proof points have an allowed claim status.
- Required qualifiers are included exactly.
- No confidential, restricted, internal-only, expired, or superseded information is included.
- The next step is specific.
- Tone, spelling, grammar, dates, time zones, links, attachments, and contact information are correct.
- The correct approved version is being shared.
- Required approval is documented.

## 18. Ownership and Accountability

Every employee who communicates externally on behalf of Hire'in is responsible for following this standard.

- **BD Representative or Account Owner:** Owns client context, opportunity quality, discovery, personalization, follow-through, and final human review.
- **BD Manager:** Owns pipeline quality, coaching, qualification discipline, approval escalation, and adherence to win profiles.
- **Knowledge Owner responsibility during the current phase:** The CEO or designated business leader owns source review, proof-point status, approved wording, restrictions, supersession, and scheduled review. This operating responsibility does not require a new technical role.
- **Brand or Content Owner:** Owns approved visual standards, messaging consistency, and master client-facing assets.
- **Super Admin or System Owner:** Owns access, configuration, auditability, and technical enforcement.
- **CEO or Authorized Leadership:** Owns strategic positioning, material external claims, pricing exceptions, partnerships, and final escalation decisions.

Initiative is encouraged, but external communication must remain within approved standards. When uncertain, pause external sharing and request review.

## 19. Final Standard

Every client interaction should communicate one message:

**Hire'in Solutions understands the need, focuses on situations where it can add credible value, acts with discipline, communicates clearly, and takes ownership of delivery.**

- "Qualified clinicians in hours, not weeks"
- "24/7 responsiveness" or around-the-clock support
- Nationwide deployment or multi-state scale claims
- Guaranteed placements, guaranteed starts, or guaranteed compliance
- Joint Commission certification or alignment claims without precise supporting evidence
- HIPAA, FISMA, state regulatory, or other compliance claims that imply certification or legal assurance

- Government-contract or certified-engagement claims without contract and certification evidence
- Use of client logos without written authorization
- Any performance statistic without a defined calculation, time period, population, source, and owner
- Any statement generated from internal financial projections, fill-rate assumptions, ROI models, or future plans
